# The grill

Pull out every decision the site needs, with as little effort from them as possible.

## How to ask

- Four rounds, three to five questions each. Number the questions. Skip anything they've already answered.
- Put your recommended answer under every question, so they can reply "yes to all" or only fix the ones they disagree with.
- If your tool can show multiple choice questions, use it.
- Push back on vague answers. "Modern", "clean", "professional", and "make it pop" mean nothing on their own. Ask which website looks that way to them.
- Find facts yourself. If they give a URL, read it before you ask about it.
- When nothing is left to decide, sum up the answers in five lines and get a yes before moving on.

Format each question like this:

> **Q1. The goal.** When someone lands on your site, what's the one thing you want them to do?
> Recommended: book a call with you.

## Round 1: the basics

1. **What you do.** In one sentence, what does the business do, and for who?
   Recommended: draft the sentence yourself from what they've told you, then ask them to fix it.
2. **The goal.** What's the one action a visitor should take?
   Recommended: book a call. Almost every service business wins on this.
3. **The customer.** Who's visiting, and what are they worried about when they land?
   Recommended: suggest the most likely customer and their biggest worry, based on the business.
4. **Current site.** Do they have a website now? Get the URL.
   Recommended: if they do, read it before round 2 so you don't ask what it already answers.

## Round 2: look and feel

1. **Colors.** Do they already have brand colors (hex codes, a logo, a sign, a truck wrap), or should you pick?
   Recommended: keep the brand color for recognition and build the rest of the palette around it.
2. **Three feeling words.** How should a visitor feel in the first five seconds? Calm, bold, premium, friendly, fast, local...
   Recommended: suggest three based on the business and the customer.
3. **Sites they love.** One to three websites they like the look of, from any industry. Apple, Nike, Airbnb, a competitor, anything. What do they like about each one?
   Recommended: if they can't think of any, suggest two from `inspiration.md` that fit their feeling words.
4. **Anything they hate.** Colors, styles, or sites they never want to look like.

## Round 3: proof and content

1. **Services or offers.** Get every one. The site shows all of them.
2. **Proof.** Real reviews, case studies, before and after photos, client logos, awards, a team photo. What do they actually have?
   Recommended: for a small business, a real team photo and three real reviews beat everything else.
3. **Location.** Do customers come to them, or do they serve an area?
   Recommended: if they're local, add a map, directions, and local business schema.
4. **Response time.** How fast do they answer a call request or a message?
   Recommended: promise something they can keep, like "We reply within one business day."
5. **Questions customers always ask.** Get five. They become the FAQ.

## Round 4: the build

1. **Where it gets built.** Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Lovable, v0, Bolt, Framer, Webflow, WordPress?
   Recommended: whatever they already use. If nothing, Next.js with Tailwind and shadcn/ui, deployed on Vercel.
2. **Booking.** How do people book the call: a scheduling link (Cal.com, Calendly), a form, or a phone number?
   Recommended: a scheduling link, plus a phone number for people who'd rather call.
3. **Pages.** Which pages do they need?
   Recommended: Home, Services, About, Contact, Privacy policy, Terms, a thank you page, and a custom 404. Add a refund policy if they sell online.
4. **Data.** Do they sell online, collect emails, or want analytics?
   Recommended: only collect what the goal needs. The answer decides which legal pages and consent steps are required.

## The default page order

Use this unless their goal or references say otherwise. Each section answers the visitor's next question.

1. **Hero:** what they do, who it's for, and one button for the goal (three words or less). Under it, one trust line, like the response time promise or a real rating.
2. **Proof strip:** real reviews or client logos.
3. **Services:** every offer, one plain sentence each.
4. **How it works:** three steps, and the last step is the call.
5. **Deeper proof:** case studies, before and afters, longer reviews.
6. **About:** the team photo and why they do this work.
7. **FAQ:** five real questions that open and close.
8. **Final call to action:** the same button, the response time promise, real contact details, and a map if they're local.

On phones, keep a sticky button for the goal at the bottom of the screen.
